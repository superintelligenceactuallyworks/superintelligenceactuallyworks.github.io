What Is Superintelligence, Actually? AGI vs ASI Explained

Video 01 · sources as of 14 Oct 2026

All figures shown on screen carry a source tag. v2 was revised on 14 Oct 2026. Benchmark numbers are snapshots dated by their source ("as of <month 2026>"), and newer models may score differently.

  • Grace, K. et al. (2024). "Thousands of AI Authors on the Future of AI." arXiv:2401.02843; JAIR vol. 84 (2025). https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.02843
    • 2,778 researchers surveyed, Oct 2023. Aggregate HLMI ("unaided machines can accomplish every task better and more cheaply than human workers"): 10% by 2027, 50% by 2047, which is 13 years earlier than the 2022 survey (2060).
    • Full Automation of Labor: 50% by 2116. 57.8% gave ≥5% chance to extremely bad outcomes (e.g. human extinction); 38–51% gave ≥10%, depending on question framing.
  • Morris, M. R. et al. (2024). "Position: Levels of AGI for Operationalizing Progress on the Path to AGI." ICML 2024. arXiv:2311.02462. https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.02462
    • Performance (0–5: No AI, Emerging, Competent, Expert, Virtuoso, Superhuman) × generality (Narrow/General) matrix. 2023 frontier LLMs (ChatGPT, Bard, Llama 2) = Emerging AGI. Competent AGI and above: not yet achieved. Superhuman Narrow examples: AlphaFold, AlphaZero, StockFish.
    • Matrix cell examples in the video are paraphrased and shortened from Table 1.
  • OpenAI Charter (2018). https://openai.com/charter. AGI = "highly autonomous systems that outperform humans at most economically valuable work."
  • Hendrycks, D. et al. (2025). "A Definition of AGI." arXiv:2510.18212. https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.18212 · https://www.agidefinition.ai
    • As of Oct 2026, no updated official scores have been published. The scores shown date from Oct 2025. A separate AAAI-2026 paper ("A Coherence-Based Measure of AGI") proposes other ways of aggregating the scores. It is not used in the video.
    • AGI = matching the cognitive versatility and proficiency of a well-educated adult; 10 CHC-based domains, 10% each. GPT-4: 27%, GPT-5: 57%. arXiv v1 listed 58%, with working memory at 5% instead of 4%; the video uses the current 57%. Per-domain values come from Table 1. Long-term memory storage = 0% for both.
  • Bostrom, N. (2014). Superintelligence: Paths, Dangers, Strategies. Oxford University Press.
    • Ch. 2 definition: "any intellect that greatly exceeds the cognitive performance of humans in virtually all domains of interest."
    • Ch. 3: speed / collective / quality superintelligence (definitions quoted on screen); "Direct and indirect reach"; "Sources of advantage for digital intelligence" (neurons ~200 Hz vs GHz processors; copyability).
    • The 1000× ⇒ "a year in ~8.76 hours" figure is our own illustrative arithmetic (8,760 h / 1000), labelled ILLUSTRATIVE on screen.
  • Good, I. J. (1965). "Speculations Concerning the First Ultraintelligent Machine." Advances in Computers 6. The intelligence-explosion idea, quoted in Bostrom 2014. Self-improvement loop labelled SPECULATION.
  • Epoch AI. "Training compute of frontier AI models grows by 4–5x per year" (2024). https://epoch.ai/publications/training-compute-of-frontier-ai-models-grows-by-4-5x-per-year
  • Epoch AI. "Trends in AI." https://epoch.ai/trends (accessed 14 Oct 2026, figures unchanged from v1). Frontier LM training compute ~5×/yr since 2020 (~10,000× total); training cost ~3.5×/yr; largest data centres approaching ~1 GW.
  • Frymire, L. & Owen, D. (Epoch AI, 2025). "Training compute growth is driven by larger clusters, longer training, and better hardware." https://epoch.ai/data-insights/training-compute-decomposition. Cluster size ~1.7×/yr, training time ~1.5×/yr, hardware FLOP/s ~1.4×/yr.
  • METR, Time Horizon 1.1, "Task-Completion Time Horizons of Frontier AI Models." https://metr.org/time-horizons/. Raw data: https://metr.org/assets/benchmark_results_1_1.yaml (downloaded 14 Oct 2026). The page was last updated with data on 8 May 2026 and was marked "no longer actively updated" on 8 Sep 2026.
    • Doubling time across all models (stitched): 187.8 days, i.e. about 6 months. From 2023 on: 128.7 days, about 4 months (95% CI 104–158 days).
    • Claude Opus 4.6: p50 = 11.98 h (95% CI 5.28–60.56 h), p80 = 1.16 h. Claude Mythos Preview (early): p50 = 17.4 h, above METR's 16-hour reliability limit, so it is plotted as a hollow marker and not quoted.
    • The on-screen chart plots METR's TH1.1 point estimates for 26 models. The dashed line is the 188-day doubling, anchored at GPT-2.
    • METR, "Time Horizon 1.1" (29 Jan 2026): https://metr.substack.com/p/2026-1-29-time-horizon-1-1
  • Kwa, T. et al. (METR, 2025). "Measuring AI Ability to Complete Long Tasks." arXiv:2503.14499. https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14499
    • 50% time horizon doubling ~7 months (2019–2025). Claude 3.7 Sonnet ~50 min (v2, early 2025). 80% horizons are ~5× shorter. Messier tasks score lower.
    • METR note "Clarifying limitations of time horizon" (Jan 2026): https://metr.org/notes/2026-01-22-time-horizon-limitations/ (wide error bars).
    • The on-screen curve is a schematic of the reported trend anchored at ~50 min. It does not plot individual model data points.
  • ARC Prize Foundation (24 Mar 2025). "Announcing ARC-AGI-2 and ARC Prize 2025." https://arcprize.org/blog/announcing-arc-agi-2-and-arc-prize-2025. Launch table (pass@2, semi-private): human panel 100% (≥2 humans per task) / 60% average; o3-preview-low 4%; o1-pro 1%; o3-mini-high 0%; GPT-4.5 0%. ARC-AGI-1: o3-preview-low 75.7%, ~$200/task. (* = partial estimates at launch.)
  • ARC Prize (25 Mar 2026). "Announcing ARC-AGI-3." https://arcprize.org/blog/arc-agi-3-launch. Humans 100%; frontier AI 0.51% at launch.
  • ARC Prize Foundation (2026). "ARC-AGI-3: A New Challenge for Frontier Agentic Intelligence." arXiv:2603.24621. Table 2, launch scores on the semi-private set: Opus 4.6 (Max) 0.50%, Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview 0.40%, GPT-5.4 (High) 0.20%, Grok-4.20 0.10%.
  • ARC Prize (3 Sep 2026). "OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra on ARC-AGI-3." https://arcprize.org/blog/astra. Semi-private set: 62.7% with the Standard harness (max effort, $26,098) and 99.9% with the Provider Adapter harness (high effort, $18,817). The foundation states: "we are not claiming that it is AGI."
    • Not used: third-party aggregators list ARC-AGI-2 scores of about 95% (e.g. for GPT-6 Astra). I could not read that number on arcprize.org itself, because the leaderboard is rendered by JavaScript, so it is left out of the video.
  • ARC Prize (Dec 2024). "OpenAI o3 Breakthrough High Score on ARC-AGI-Pub." https://arcprize.org/blog/oai-o3-pub-breakthrough
  • Chollet, F. (2019). "On the Measure of Intelligence." arXiv:1911.01547. Background framing for skill-acquisition and generality.

Executive Order 14434 (cold open, segment eo) — verified on primary text

  • Executive Order 14434 of September 29, 2026, "Inaugurating the Era of Super Intelligence," 91 FR 63129–63130 (2 Oct 2026), FR Doc 2026-20321. https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/02/2026-20321/inaugurating-the-era-of-super-intelligence (full text: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/text/2026/10/02/2026-20321.txt). White House: https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/09/inaugurating-the-era-of-super-intelligence/
    • Sec. 2(a), exact: agencies "shall use `Super Intelligence'' and SI'' in place of Artificial Intelligence'' and `AI''" in official correspondence, public communications, websites, reports, policy documents (to the maximum extent permitted by law).
    • Sec. 3(a), exact: the terms "mean the technologies and systems encompassed by the term ``artificial intelligence'' as defined in section 9401(3) of title 15, United States Code" → narration: "Its legal definition? The same one AI already had."

Bostrom examples (speed / collective / quality) — Bostrom 2014, ch. 3 "Forms of superintelligence"

  • Speed: "An emulation operating at a speed of ten thousand times that of a biological brain would be able to read a book in a few seconds and write a PhD thesis in an afternoon." (replaces v3's own ×1000 arithmetic)
  • Collective: "Firms, work teams, gossip networks, advocacy groups, academic communities, countries, even humankind as a whole, can ... be viewed as loosely defined 'systems'" (collective intelligence examples).
  • Quality: Bostrom contrasts humans with "chimpanzees and dolphins intensely trained by human instructors" vs. "small, isolated hunter-gatherer bands", attributing the gap to "specific features of our brain architecture".
