Can AI Read an Analog Clock? 50.6% in Mar 2026, 83.3% by Oct (ClockBench)

Short · 19 Oct 2026 · full video: https://youtu.be/m6imT0CA9hQ

  • [S1] Stanford HAI, AI Index Report 2026, ch. 2 Technical Performance, Chapter Highlights #7 (printed p. 71 / PDF p. 72): https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2026-ai-index-report/technical-performance · https://hai.stanford.edu/assets/files/ai_index_report_2026.pdf
    • Quote: "Gemini Deep Think scored 35 points (gold) at the 2025 IMO, working end to end in natural language within the 4.5-hour time limit"
  • [S2] Stanford HAI, AI Index Report 2026, §2.4 Highlight "Time Understanding in MLLMs" (p. 96) + Figure 2.4.8 (p. 97): https://hai.stanford.edu/assets/files/ai_index_report_2026.pdf
    • Quote: "ClockBench (Safar, 2025) … 180 clock designs and 720 questions. Humans read correctly formatted clocks correctly 90.1% of the time, while GPT-5.4 High, the top model, reached 50.6% in March 2026 (Figure 2.4.8)." Note: the report's Top Takeaways (p. 9) says 50.1%, and Fig. 2.4.8 labels the human baseline 90.70%. We use the chapter figure 50.6% (named model + date).
  • [S3] ClockBench leaderboard (Alek Safar), live: https://clockbench.ai
    • Quote: Leaderboard as of 18 Oct 2026: #1 Gemini 4 Argon High 83.3%; Average Human Performance 90.7%; Top Human 95%; GPT-5.4 High 50.6% (rank 10). Snapshot; the leaderboard changes.
  • Note: The AI Index itself is inconsistent (50.1% on p. 9 vs 50.6% on pp. 71/96; humans 90.1% in text vs 90.70% in Fig. 2.4.8). We use 50.6% (named model, dated) and 'about 90%' for humans.
  • Note: 83.3% is a live-leaderboard snapshot (18 Oct 2026); re-check clockbench.ai on publish day and update if the top score changed.

AI Data Centre Power: ~30 GW, About New York State's Peak (Epoch AI Estimate)

    • Quote: "Total AI data center power capacity reached approximately 30 GW in the last quarter of 2025—comparable to peak power usage in New York State"; table: 2025-12-31 datacenter power 31.24 GW (extrapolated); "rated power capacity of leading AI chips … We apply a ~2.5x multiplier … servers, cooling, and infrastructure"; "New York State, at around 31 GW (2025 estimate)"; "TDP represents maximum rated power draw rather than actual consumption".

Can Machines Think? Turing's 1950 Imitation Game (Turing Test)

Short · 19 Oct 2026 · full video: https://youtu.be/m6imT0CA9hQ

  • [S1] Turing, A. M. (Oct 1950). "Computing Machinery and Intelligence". Mind, New Series, LIX(236): 433–460 (28 pages). doi:10.1093/mind/LIX.236.433 (the OUP/doi.org pages answer HTTP 403 to automated checkers, so we link JSTOR and a full-text copy): https://www.jstor.org/stable/2251299 · full text: https://www.cs.colostate.edu/~howe/cs440/csroo/yr2015fa/more_assignments/turing.pdf
    • Quote: p. 433 (§1): "I PROPOSE to consider the question, 'Can machines think?' … I shall replace the question by another, which is closely related to it and is expressed in relatively unambiguous words. The new form of the problem can be described in terms of a game which we call the 'imitation game'." p. 434: "the answers should be written, or better still, typewritten. The ideal arrangement is to have a teleprinter communicating between the two rooms." "What will happen when a machine takes the part of A in this game?" p. 442 (§6): "The original question, 'Can machines think?' I believe to be too meaningless to deserve discussion." "I believe that in about fifty years' time it will be possible to programme computers … to make them play the imitation game so well that an average interrogator will not have more than 70 per cent. chance of making the right identification after five minutes of questioning."
  • Note: Simplification for MM: in Turing's set-up the game is first man vs woman; the machine then 'takes the part of A'. VO says 'two hidden players, one of them a machine' (the standard reading).
  • Note: OUP (academic.oup.com) and doi.org return 403 to curl; JSTOR stable URL and the colostate PDF return 200 (checked 18 Oct 2026).
